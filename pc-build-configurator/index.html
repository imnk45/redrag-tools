Why Compatibility Checking Matters

One of the most common mistakes new PC builders make is buying incompatible parts. For example, pairing a DDR5-only CPU with a DDR4 motherboard, or selecting a GPU that is too long for the case. Our tool automatically checks CPU socket compatibility (AM4, AM5, LGA1700, LGA1851), validates RAM type matching, verifies GPU clearance against case dimensions, and calculates whether your PSU has enough wattage with proper headroom. We recommend 25% PSU headroom above your system's peak draw for stability and future upgrades.

Understanding the 2026 PC Component Landscape

The PC hardware market in 2026 is more competitive than ever. AMD continues to dominate gaming with their X3D processors like the Ryzen 7 9800X3D and 9850X3D, which use 3D V-Cache technology to deliver exceptional gaming performance with massive L3 cache. Intel's Core Ultra 200 series (Arrow Lake) on the LGA1851 socket brings improved efficiency and DDR5-8000 support, making them strong contenders for productivity and workstation builds. On the GPU side, NVIDIA's RTX 50-series (Blackwell architecture) leads in ray tracing and DLSS 4 with Multi-Frame Generation, while AMD's RX 9000 series (RDNA 4) offers excellent rasterization performance and competitive pricing with FSR 4 support.

For budget builds under $1,000, the AMD Ryzen 5 7500F paired with an RX 7700 XT or RTX 5060 delivers outstanding 1080p and entry 1440p gaming. The AM5 platform is particularly valuable here because it offers a clear upgrade path to faster Ryzen 9000 CPUs without changing your motherboard. For mid-range builds between $1,500 and $2,000, the Ryzen 7 7800X3D remains the gaming king, especially when paired with the RX 9070 XT or RTX 5070 Ti for high-refresh 1440p gaming. Enthusiasts looking at $3,000+ builds should consider the Ryzen 7 9800X3D or Ryzen 9 9950X3D with an RTX 5080 or RTX 5090 for uncompromising 4K Ultra performance.

How We Calculate Performance and Wattage

Our PC Build Configurator uses a comprehensive database of real-world component specifications to estimate performance and power draw. CPU TDP values are taken from manufacturer specifications, while GPU power consumption is based on typical gaming loads rather than peak synthetic stress tests. We calculate total system wattage by adding CPU TDP, GPU TDP, and approximately 75W for motherboard, RAM, storage, and fans combined. The PSU recommendation adds 25% headroom on top of this total to ensure stable operation and accommodate transient power spikes that modern GPUs like the RTX 5090 can exhibit.

FPS estimates are derived from aggregated benchmark data across popular titles including Cyberpunk 2077, Black Myth: Wukong, Call of Duty, Fortnite, and Valorant. These are approximate values at Ultra settings without upscaling technologies like DLSS or FSR. Enabling DLSS 4 or FSR 4 can significantly boost these numbers — sometimes by 50% or more — making even mid-range cards viable for higher resolutions.

About This Tool

The REDRAG PC Build Configurator is a free, browser-based calculator designed to help gamers and PC enthusiasts plan their custom builds with confidence. Unlike other PC builders that require accounts or only work with specific retailers, our tool works entirely in your browser with no sign-up needed.

How we calculate compatibility:

  • CPU Socket Check: Validates that your selected motherboard supports your CPU's socket (AM4, AM5, LGA1700, or LGA1851).
  • RAM Type Validation: Ensures DDR4 or DDR5 memory matches both the CPU and motherboard requirements.
  • GPU Clearance Check: Compares graphics card length against the PC case's maximum GPU length specification.
  • PSU Wattage Calculation: Adds CPU TDP + GPU TDP + 75W system overhead, then applies 25% headroom for stability.
  • Cooler TDP Check: Verifies that your CPU cooler's thermal dissipation capacity exceeds the CPU's TDP rating.
  • Balance Score: Analyzes the CPU-GPU pairing to detect potential bottlenecks at different resolutions.
