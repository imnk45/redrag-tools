How to Use the Game Download Time Calculator

Wondering how long it will take to download a game before you commit your evening to it? This game download time calculator gives you a precise, real-world answer in seconds. Either pick your game from our built-in list of 40+ popular PC, PS5 and Xbox titles with their actual install sizes, or enter any custom file size. Then type your internet speed exactly as your ISP advertises it (in Mbps), choose the connection conditions that match your setup, and the calculator instantly shows your estimated download time, your effective speed in MB/s, and even the clock time your download will finish.

Important: Mbps ≠ MB/s

Internet plans are sold in megabits per second (Mbps), but launchers like Steam and Epic show download progress in megabytes per second (MB/s). Since 1 byte = 8 bits, a 100 Mbps connection has a theoretical maximum of only 12.5 MB/s — and roughly 11 MB/s in real-world conditions. Confusing these two units is the #1 reason download estimates go wrong.

The Download Time Formula

The math behind every download time calculator is simple division — the hard part is keeping the units consistent:

Download time (seconds) = (File size in MB × 8) ÷ effective speed in Mbps

Worked example: downloading a 150 GB game like Call of Duty: Black Ops 6 on a 100 Mbps connection. That's 153,600 MB × 8 = 1,228,800 megabits, divided by 90 Mbps (100 Mbps at our default real-world 90% efficiency) = 13,653 seconds, or about 3 hours 47 minutes. The same download on 500 Mbps fibre takes roughly 46 minutes, and on a 1 Gbps connection around 23 minutes.

Why Your Real Download May Take Longer

No download time calculator can promise perfection, because your connection is only half the story. The game server matters too — on launch day, millions of players hammering the same servers can slow everyone down regardless of plan speed. Wi-Fi signal loss, family members streaming 4K in the next room, VPN overhead, and an old hard drive that can't write data fast enough all eat into your speed. That's why this calculator includes connection condition presets instead of pretending you'll always hit 100% of your advertised speed — an approach recommended by networking experts who suggest using 80–90% of your quoted speed for realistic estimates.

How to Download Games Faster

  • Use Ethernet instead of Wi-Fi — a wired connection consistently delivers 95%+ of plan speed.
  • Download during off-peak hours — late night and early morning have less network congestion.
  • Pause everything else — streams, cloud backups and other downloads share your bandwidth.
  • Pick the closest download region — in Steam: Settings → Downloads → Download Region.
  • Skip the VPN while downloading — encryption and extra routing cost speed.
  • Check your storage drive — a slow HDD can bottleneck a 500 Mbps+ fibre connection.

About This Tool

The REDRAG Game Download Time Calculator is a free, browser-based calculator designed to help gamers get an honest answer to one simple question: how long will this download take? It also includes a built-in internet speed test that measures your real download speed and feeds it straight into the calculator.

How we calculate:

  • We convert your file size to megabytes using binary units (1 GB = 1024 MB), matching how Windows and game launchers display sizes.
  • We convert your internet speed to megabits per second (Mbps) regardless of the unit you enter — Kbps, Mbps, Gbps or even MB/s.
  • We apply a real-world efficiency factor: 100% theoretical, 90% for a typical wired connection (our default), 75% for Wi-Fi, or 50% for congested peak hours.
  • Download time = (size in MB × 8) ÷ effective speed in Mbps, then formatted into days, hours, minutes and seconds.
  • Game install sizes come from our curated dataset of 40+ popular titles and are approximate — patches, DLC and texture packs can grow them over time.
  • The built-in speed test downloads multi-megabyte payloads from multiple public test servers, keeps samples that ran at least 1 second, and returns the median — a robust method that resists one-off spikes.
