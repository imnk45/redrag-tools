How to Convert Mouse Sensitivity Between Games (The Exact Method)

A mouse sensitivity converter translates your in-game sensitivity from one game into the equivalent value in another, so the same physical mouse movement produces the same camera rotation everywhere. Every game engine uses a hidden constant called yaw — the degrees your view rotates per mouse count at sensitivity 1.0. Because each engine hard-codes a different yaw, the same number means a completely different turn speed in each game. Our converter uses verified yaw values for every supported game, which is why the results match your muscle memory instead of just feeling "close".

The Formula We Use

Target Sens = Source Sens × (Source Yaw ÷ Target Yaw)

This preserves your cm/360° — the centimetres of mousepad needed for a full 360° turn — which is the universal standard for comparing sensitivity across games. DPI cancels out of the conversion unless you genuinely use different DPIs in the two games.

What Are cm/360°, in/360° and eDPI?

cm/360° is the physical distance your mouse must travel for a full rotation: cm/360 = 914.4 ÷ (DPI × Sens × Yaw). Two players with the same cm/360 turn exactly the same amount for the same hand movement, regardless of game — that is why pros compare this number instead of raw sensitivity. eDPI (DPI × in-game sens) is the standard way to compare players within the same game. Use the FOV-Aware mode on this tool when your two games use different fields of view — it applies focal-length scaling so your crosshair-speed matches at the centre of the screen, the same free method aim trainers use.

About This Tool

The REDRAG Mouse Sensitivity Converter is a free, browser-based calculator designed to help FPS gamers carry their muscle memory across games without guesswork.

How we calculate:

  • 360° Match mode: target sens = source sens × (source yaw ÷ target yaw). Exact by definition — your cm/360 stays identical.
  • FOV-Aware mode: additionally multiplies by tan(source FOV ÷ 2) ÷ tan(target FOV ÷ 2) to match crosshair-level aim speed when games use different fields of view.
  • cm/360: 914.4 ÷ (DPI × sens × yaw)  •  in/360: 360 ÷ (DPI × sens × yaw)  •  eDPI: DPI × sens
  • Yaw values are engine constants verified against Mouse-Sensitivity.com, MouseTester.io and community testing (last reviewed October 2026).
  • Games with non-linear sliders (Fortnite, Rainbow Six Siege, PUBG) are clearly flagged as approximate — we would rather show an honest range than a fake exact number.
